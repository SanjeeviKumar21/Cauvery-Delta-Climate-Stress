**What changed between summer and Aadi Pattam in the Cauvery Delta?**

I analyzed daily gridded weather data for eight locations across the Cauvery Delta agricultural belt and adjoining coast, comparing 2023–2026 in R.

A key result was the **July–August 2026 establishment window**:

- Sampled rainfall: **52.9 mm**, compared with **91.3 mm in 2025** (42% lower).
- FAO-56 reference ET₀: **379.6 mm**, compared with **354.8 mm in 2025** (7% higher).
- Days when the eight-location mean maximum temperature exceeded **35°C**: **42**, compared with **24** in 2025.

I also compared **April–June**, the period preceding Aadi sowing. In these sampled grids, 2026 had the lowest pre-Aadi rainfall of the four years, making the progression from summer conditions into crop establishment worth closer examination.

What matters here is not simply how much rainfall occurred across a season, but **when water arrived and the atmospheric demand crops faced during establishment**.

This has practical implications for weather-aware sowing, soil-moisture monitoring, irrigation planning and protecting young plants against heat exposure. The next step is testing those approaches with actual field observations—not assuming the model already proves crop damage or water savings.

These are **gridded weather estimates, not farm measurements**, and four years alone do not establish a long-term climate-change trend.
